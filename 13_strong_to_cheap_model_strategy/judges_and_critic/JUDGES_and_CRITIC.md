# Judge panel and completeness critic

Three Sonnet 5.5 judges (personas: sceptical CFO; ML engineer who has shipped small-model systems with verifiers; STEM teacher-producer) scored the four architect plans on quality-parity realism, cost-reduction realism, implementation effort, maintainability, edge-case coverage and ExamLeaf fit. An Opus 5.5 completeness critic then reviewed the first synthesis; its must-fix list was addressed in the final version.

## Judges

### Judge 1

**Winner:** verifier-and-cascade-first

As a sceptical CFO, I note first what none of the four plans has: a single metered token or a single rendered frame. Every dollar figure is an assumption, including the headline Opus baselines, and V1's own dollars do not reconcile with list prices. Three of the four quote 20-98x LLM-line savings by comparing against a favourable baseline: free-form Opus in lens 1, bespoke V1-style runs in lens 2, naive uncached Opus in lens 4. Only two plans give the real like-for-like ratio, and it is about 5-13x once tokenizer inflation (about 30-36%), medium-effort output, retries and escalation are priced in. Total per-lesson cost moves only 2-5% (about 1.26x at best) because human review is $26.67 of about $34. So '40-100x cheaper videos' is not achievable. 'Opus-grade animation for roughly the same total cost as today' is.

I pick verifier-and-cascade-first (43) over economics-and-ops-first (42), and the two are close. Lens 2 gets a high cost-realism score because it states the like-for-like ratio (5-10x), gives a break-even (about 240 lessons) and an escalation ceiling (15%), and tracks the risk that matters most (verifier Goodhart, via an escape-rate metric). It also builds the one asset every other plan needs: executable verifiers that make cheap-model failure cheap and safe. Its weaknesses are Haiku still writing glue code and the unverified vision-judge assumption. Lens 4 is the most honest on cost (13x, -4% total) but is vaguer on how the kit gets built and tested. Lens 1 has the best structural defence against crappy output and the best edge-case closure, but its 98x claim is a strawman, it takes the most work, and it caps quality at the library's coverage. Lens 3 has the best experimental design but the weakest quality guarantee, since it depends on Haiku copying exemplars, and its cost model is the least conservative.

Recommendation: adopt lens 2 as the spine, then fold in lens 1's closure rule and no-free-text constraints (as template-parameter schemas, not a full DSL), lens 3's D/E/F/G arm ladder to test whether docs help at all, and lens 4's cost-per-accepted-lesson metric and review-minutes programme. Week 1 must install Node 22 and FFmpeg, render a scene, and meter real tokens before any ratio is believed. Present 5-13x on the LLM line and a review-time cut as the realistic goals, not 40-100x.

### Judge 2

**Winner:** economics-and-ops-first

The economics-and-ops-first plan wins narrowly (43, against 41, 39 and 39) because it is the most honest about what the cost claims mean, and the user's real question is whether a 40-100x saving is achievable. Every plan, once corrected, lands in the same place. Like-for-like (Opus and Haiku given the same templated or slot-fill task), the saving is about 13x in Lens 4, 5-10x in Lens 2, about 15-22x in Lens 1 and Lens 3, and falls to about 4.5x if 15% of scenes escalate. The 40-100x figures appear only when Opus writing bespoke free-form scenes is compared with Haiku filling a constrained spec. That saving comes from the architecture (templates, slots, verifiers), not from swapping the model. All four plans also agree that the LLM line is about 5% of the roughly $34 per-lesson cost, so total cost moves only about 2-4%, and the real lever is the 85 human review minutes ($26.67). Only Lens 4 makes that its organising principle, with cost per accepted lesson as the metric, a break-even analysis, and a review-minutes track.

Lens 1 has the strongest quality mechanism. It makes errors inexpressible (no free text, glyphs, times or colours), has the best edge-case closure discipline, and fits ExamLeaf's fixtures and script-hash rules best. It loses points on effort (3) and on a 98x headline that swaps the workload as well as the model, with an Opus baseline labelled high effort but priced at the low-effort rate. Lens 2 has the best monitoring (escape rate, judge calibration, refusal routing, best-of-N only with a real verifier) but the highest verifier-building burden and an unproven Haiku-class judge. Lens 3 has the best experimental design for the user's own question (the D/E/F/G ablation of docs vs exemplars vs verifiers) and the best prompt/cache layout, but it relies on Haiku writing free code and its 22x omits effort inflation and per-archetype cache writes.

Recommended synthesis: use Lens 4 as the program frame and decision metric (cost per accepted lesson, 15% escalation trigger, review-minutes track). Take Lens 1's slot-fill/DSL as the architecture for Haiku calls, so output tokens and failure modes shrink. Take Lens 2's verifier-only state advancement, escape-rate metric and refusal routing as the gate and monitoring design. Take Lens 3's D/E/F/G ablation as the week-2-to-4 experiment and its cache-first prompt layout. The ablation also tests whether long documentation helps at all. Prerequisite for all of it: install Node 22 and FFmpeg and prove a deterministic render in week 1. Meter real token and attempt numbers before accepting any ratio, and quote 10-20x on the LLM line, with 2-4% on total cost, until review minutes are shown to fall.

### Judge 3

**Winner:** verifier-and-cascade-first

Totals are 44 (verifier-and-cascade), 43 (economics-and-ops), 42 (exemplar-and-prompt), 38 (library-and-DSL). The margins are small, so the pick turns on what each plan gets wrong.

Cost claims first, since I was told to be harsh. The DSL plan's 98x compares Opus writing free-form code with Haiku filling a JSON slot spec. That is mostly architecture, not model price. The exemplar plan's 22x gives Haiku the same output tokens per scene as Opus, and that is optimistic given the extra thinking tokens at medium effort [N9] and the tokenizer inflation [N2, N42]. The verifier plan and the economics plan both state that the fair like-for-like saving is about 5-13x, and that the LLM line is only about 4-5% of per-video cost because the $26.67 of human review dominates. Those two are the credible ones. Nothing in any plan supports a flat 40-100x on total cost. At best the 40-100x holds on the LLM line against bespoke strong-model generation.

I picked the verifier-and-cascade plan because it ties the user's goal (cheap models that do not produce crappy videos) to checks that run in code. Its thesis is that cheap models will still fail, so failures must be caught by deterministic verifiers (fixture diff for science values, double-render hash, layout and safe-zone QA, timing) and escalated only when a verifier fails. That matches the evidence on Haiku: weak agentic performance [N4], early stops [N9], false 'done' claims [N10], tool skips [N13], refusals [N16], and repeated sampling helping only when a real verifier exists [N88-N92]. It also measures the escape rate (human-found defects that passed all verifiers), which is the number a teacher cares about.

Its gaps are taste and script-specific layout. The verifiers cannot judge whether a scene teaches well or looks good, and Indic and Assamese handling is thin. Those gaps are covered by transplants from the other plans.

Ideas to pull in from the losers:
1. From DSL: the closure order (default, validator, schema, exemplar, prose) and no free on-screen text. All text should be spans of the approved script and all science visuals should come from the fixture registry. Apply this to the templates, without building a full DSL.
2. From exemplar: negative cards built from real Haiku failures, Indic shaping and per-script text-fit checks, a 'strings subset of brief' check, an 'unknown archetype, escalate' path, and an adversarial scene set.
3. From economics: cost per accepted lesson as the headline metric, the review-minutes lever (the only thing that moves total cost materially), Reels derived deterministically from the approved lesson, and an escalation-rate trigger that treats a high rate as a template defect.

Week 1 is the same blocker in every plan. There is no FFmpeg or Node 22 on this Mac and no real renders, so none of the verifier thresholds mean anything until a scene renders deterministically.

## Completeness critic

**Verdict:** The architecture is sound and the arithmetic mostly checks out. I re-derived every row in 7.2–7.7 and found one cap error. But several conclusions rest on wrong API assumptions or on biased inputs. (1) The prescribed "adaptive thinking + forced tool_choice" setup breaks. It returns HTTP 400 on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. On Haiku 5.5 the request is accepted, but forcing the tool skips thinking. (2) The ×1.36 tokenizer penalty is applied to Haiku only. Opus and Sonnet 5.5 already use the newer tokenizer, so this inflates Haiku cost. (3) The [V1] dollar rates behind the headline 55x–900x ratios and the 58-lesson break-even are impossible at list prices. (4) The script and text work is moved to Haiku with no eval behind it. (5) The holdout set is too small and the rater hours are undercounted, so the go/no-go is not credible as written. Fix the 12 items below before this goes to the founder or the academic team.

**Missing (before revision):**
- No eval arm for the text tasks the cost model moves to Haiku: section script writing in English, Hinglish, Hindi and Assamese, the Super Teacher voice, beat segmentation, Reel hooks and captions. The Haiku-system and cascade lines run 200K/38K of text per lesson on Haiku with no parity check. The experiment matrix covers scenes only.
- Render and compute cost line. V3 renders every candidate twice, k=3 sampling multiplies renders, and V4/V5 need headless DOM sampling at many timestamps. The plan keeps 'render/storage/other $4.65' fixed and never estimates render minutes per 600 s lesson or wall-clock throughput for 1,000 lessons.
- Human and programme costs are left out of the $1,250 and the break-even: about 40 engineer hours (likely 2–4x too low for 14 templates, V0–V8, ledger, controller, run wrapper, anonymiser, QA-report UI and 40 scene packages); rater hours (about $18.83/h at the project's review rate); and academic time to approve the 40 scene-package script excerpts, fixtures, thresholds and templates.
- Setup cost for the 40 scene packages in week 1: each needs an approved script excerpt, a real cue sheet built from TTS word timings, and validated fixtures. That means TTS runs and academic sign-off, neither budgeted nor scheduled.
- Ongoing per-lesson costs are missing from 7.6: the weekly 5% Opus-templated shadow run (about $0.23 per lesson, about 19% of the $1.21 cascade-full LLM line), Friday trace-mining sessions (up to 5 × $8 a week), the monthly template backlog, and re-running holdout on every Kit or model change.
- Tokenizer inflation on Indic text. [N2] and [N42] measure English and YAML against Haiku 4.5. Devanagari, Assamese and Hinglish counts on ExamLeaf prompts are not measured, for Claude or for the open models, yet they drive both the 60K guard and the cost.
- Haiku at higher effort is not a rung in the escalation ladder. Haiku high or xhigh may clear an archetype more cheaply than Sonnet medium. C4 sweeps only low, medium and high, and not xhigh or max.
- Per-message effort (beta, supported on Haiku 5.5 with thinking on) lets effort change without invalidating the cache. The plan does not use it, and instead builds separate high-effort prefixes.
- Preserved-thinking constraint on repair loops. Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5 return 400 if thinking blocks are replayed after system, tools or earlier messages are edited (enforced for accounts created on or after 2026-08-31). The controller must keep the history append-only or drop thinking blocks. The plan does not say which.
- Agentic harness overhead in the bespoke Haiku arms (C0–C2). Running in Claude Code or the Agent SDK adds its own system and tool prompt, and context grows with each turn. Per-turn prompts can cross 60K and then 100K, the Haiku price step. The plan has no compaction rule and no per-turn guard.
- The user asked to experiment with 'Opus 5.5 Ultra'. The strategy bans ultracode outright without offering a capped probe or explaining the trade-off to the user against that request.
- The Chinese and open-model section names no concrete shortlist. It should separate hosted Chinese APIs (where jurisdiction and data retention apply) from self-hosted open weights (needed for the fine-tuning path). It also lacks an Indic-language quality check, beyond tokenizer counts, for those models.

**Wrong or unsupported (before revision):**
- Forced tool_choice. Exec point 2.6 step 2, 5.3, D6 and protocol 4.2 prescribe 'adaptive thinking, forced tool_choice' for every templated tier. On Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, a tool_choice of any or tool returns HTTP 400, so S3, S4 and cascade tiers 2–3 would fail as specified. On Haiku 5.5 a forced tool_choice is accepted, but the response then starts with the tool call and has no thinking block. The combination is therefore thinking-off in practice, which reintroduces the leakage and quality risks of [N13] and [N15]. [N13] lists adaptive thinking and forcing the tool as alternative fixes, not as a combination.
- Tokenizer penalty. A4 applies ×1.36 to Haiku tokens only. [N2] and [N42] compare Haiku 5.5 with Haiku 4.5, and Anthropic's own migration notes give about 30% for that comparison. Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 already use the newer tokenizer family introduced with Opus 4.7, so the same text is not 36% longer on Haiku than on Opus. Exec point 7 ('the tokenizer eats most of the 40x') and 7.2 ('16.5x because of tokenizer and verbosity') are therefore unsupported. Without the penalty, a Haiku call costs about $0.00242 and the Opus-to-Haiku ratio is about 22x per call. At lesson level Haiku costs about $0.30, making Opus templated about 15x more expensive, not 11x.
- [V1] dollars. Both runs are irreconcilable with list prices, not just the low one. $3 for 78K tokens is about $38/M, and $49 for 625K tokens is about $78/M, both above Opus's $20/M output price. Yet the bespoke figures ($22.80 and $372 per lesson), the 55x–900x and 19x–300x ratios, the 58-lesson payback and the per-scene caps all use [V1] dollar rates. A token-based upper bound (at most $20/M) gives at most about $11.85 (low) and about $95 (max) per lesson.
- Exec point 7 says the protected cascade is '4–6x' cheaper. Table 7.3 gives 3.7x (full) to 6.0x (lean).
- Exec point 8 and 7.6 give −3.9% against the $34 baseline. Against today's repriced mix ($33.49, the plan's own row), Haiku + system is −2.4%. The baseline is mixed between the two.
- Max-effort benchmarks are cited to justify a medium-effort deployment. [N38] (FrontierCode at max) and [N35] (OSWorld at max) support 'bounded, tool-verified work is where Haiku is strong', but production runs Haiku at medium. [N36] and [N39] show medium scoring sharply lower (GDPval-AA 1277 vs 1620). [N41] states no effort level.
- 2.3 says 'at most 20 archetypes'. [N44]–[N46] are per-request limits across the strict tools in one request. With routing to 3 or fewer tools per call, the library size is not capped at 20.
- Protocol 4.2 sends effort as a top-level field. The parameter is output_config.effort. Sonnet 5.5 defaults to high, not medium, so it must be set explicitly for S4.
- Pilot cap. The integration set is 4 lessons + 6 Reels × 2 runs. The cascade costs about 2 × (4 × $1.21 + 6 × $0.15) ≈ $11.5, and the Opus-templated shadow about 2 × (4 × $4.51 + 6 × $0.87) ≈ $46.5, about $58 in total against a $50 cap. The '$5 + $14' figure counts 3 lessons × 1 run.
- Rater hours. Counting the protocol's own 'what gets rated' list gives about 870 items: S0 48, S1 24, S3 72, S4 36, C0–C3 96, C4 Gold 72, C4 Holdout+Adversarial 16 × 5 × 3 efforts = 240, pilots about 260, and the week-3 compiled-gold re-rating 24. At 2 raters × 2 min that is about 58 hours, not 20–25, before pairwise comparisons and third-rater adjudication.
- Per-archetype go thresholds (≥95% final pass, ≤15% escalation, holdout within 5 points of Gold) cannot be measured with Holdout-12 spread over 14 archetypes: fewer than one sealed scene per archetype, and 5 samples of one scene are correlated. The standard error at that n is wider than the 5-point rule.
- Arm C1 trims the 'long docs' to fit under the 60K guard. It therefore does not test the founder's 'write a big manual and hand it to Haiku' idea, which [Hypothesis] 1.1 claims the experiment will settle.
- Sonnet 5.5 cache-read price. [N19], from Anthropic's pricing page, shows $0.10, which contradicts the supplied PRICES block ($0.20). The strategy cites only N84 and calls $0.20 authoritative without flagging that its own primary evidence disagrees. [N53]'s 0.05x note also reads as $0.10 for Sonnet.
- Open question 3 (minimum cacheable length for the 5.5 family) is answerable now: Anthropic documents 512 tokens for Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1. The 4,096 figure in [N54] is for Opus 4.6/4.5 and does not apply.
- Batch claims. 'Cascade full ≈ $0.61 if all batchable' ignores that repair loops and cascade escalation run in sequence, and the batch turnaround (up to 24 h) is longer than the 1-hour cache TTL. Batch also uses the 2x one-hour cache write, which the batch figures do not reflect.
- Many load-bearing citations ([N57]–[N79], [N82], [N84], [N88]–[N96]) are not in the supplied evidence list, which ends at [N56]. The SWE-bench repeated-sampling figures, the ManimBench results and the fine-tuning cost claims cannot be checked against it.

**Must-fix list (addressed in the final strategy):**
- Replace 'adaptive thinking + forced tool_choice' everywhere (2.6, 5.3, D6, protocol 4.2). On Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 use tool_choice auto with one strict tool and a prompt instruction naming it, or output_config.format with thinking on. Treat a missing call as F-TOOLSKIP and retry. On Haiku, A/B test 'forced tool (no thinking)' against 'auto + strict + thinking' in week 1, and log thinking presence in the ledger.
- Remove the Haiku-only ×1.36 tokenizer factor, or apply a measured cross-model factor to both models by running count_tokens on the same ExamLeaf prompts, including Hindi and Assamese, for claude-haiku-5-5 and claude-opus-5-5. Recompute 7.2–7.6 and exec points 7–8. The uninflated central estimate is about $0.30 for Haiku + system per lesson, so Opus templated is about 15x more expensive.
- Stop quoting the [V1]-dollar bespoke figures as point estimates. Show the token-based upper bounds next to them ($11.85 low and $95 max per lesson at $20/M) and recompute the 55x–900x ratios, the 58-lesson break-even and the per-scene caps, or label them unreconciled until week-1 metering.
- Add text-task arms (T0–T4) to the matrix: section script authoring, Hinglish and Hindi register, beat segmentation, Reel hooks and captions, with Haiku, Sonnet and the current mix. Track the effect on academic review minutes. Until a text task passes, cost the 'Haiku + system' scenario with today's text mix: $1.21 + about $0.31 of Haiku scenes ≈ $1.52 per lesson.
- Make the go/no-go statistically meaningful. Either expand the sealed holdout to at least 3 distinct scenes per archetype in scope (for example 24 for the top 8 archetypes), or make decisions per archetype family. Report Wilson confidence intervals, and require the lower bound to clear each threshold rather than the point estimate.
- Re-count rater hours from the protocol's own list (about 870 items ≈ 58 rater-hours plus pairwise). Either cut the list (for example, rate only medium-effort C4 holdout and one pilot run) or budget the hours. Price rater and engineer time into the programme total and the break-even in 7.7.
- Fix the pilot cap. 4 lessons + 6 Reels × 2 runs comes to about $58 (cascade about $11.5, Opus shadow about $46.5). Raise the cap to about $65 or halve the shadow, and correct the '$5 + $14' arithmetic. Re-total the budget.
- Add the missing ongoing cost lines to 7.6/7.7: the 5% Opus shadow (about $0.23 per lesson), Friday mining (up to $40 a week), the template backlog, holdout re-runs, and render compute for double renders and k=3 candidates. Replace the fixed $4.65 'other' figure with a render-seconds × rate estimate, to be metered in week 1.
- Correct the API details. Effort is output_config.effort (not top-level); set Sonnet 5.5 effort explicitly because its default is high. Use per-message effort (beta) instead of separate high-effort prefixes. Close open question 3: the minimum cacheable length is 512 tokens for the 5.5 family. Make repair turns append-only so replayed thinking blocks don't return 400. The 20-strict-tool limit is per request, not a library cap.
- Fix arm C1 so it tests what the founder proposed. Either give Haiku the full long manual, accepting the 100K price tier and logging the cost, or rename C1 to 'trimmed docs' and add a C1-full arm. Add a per-turn token guard and a compaction rule for the agentic bespoke Haiku arms (C0–C2), whose harness overhead and growing context can cross 60K and 100K.
- Justify Haiku at medium effort with ExamLeaf measurements, not max-effort benchmarks ([N35], [N38]). Add xhigh to the C4 effort sweep, and add a 'Haiku at higher effort' rung before Sonnet in the cascade, gated by cost per accepted scene.
- Reconcile with the user's request. Either add one hard-capped 'Opus 5.5 Ultra' ceiling probe (one hardest scene, for example a $50 cap, logged as capped if it doesn't finish), or tell the user plainly that it is excluded and why. In section 8, name a concrete shortlist of Chinese and open models, split into hosted API and self-hosted weights, each with an Indic-quality check.
