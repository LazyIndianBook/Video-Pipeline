# 15 · ExamLeaf Content Engine v2 (general, commercially agnostic, Class 1 to Masters, all subject families, any locale)

**Why this exists (15 Oct 2026).** The founder directed that the pipeline must not care what is free or paid, that the lead decides remaining blockers, and that the engine must be strong and mature enough to produce many content types (Detailed, Simplified, Recaps, Question Analysis, Shorts and more) for every stage from Class 1 to Masters in all subjects, in future. This section is the specification for that engine. It keeps everything that already works (sections 01–14) and re-expresses it as data over a single source of truth.

**The design in three separations**

1. **Truth is separated from packaging.** One canonical, source-grounded **Lesson Graph** (14 node types: claims, objectives, concept steps, analogies, misconceptions, worked items, assessment items, simplifications, activities, stories, vocabulary, passages, media, locale renderings), bound to curriculum competency GUIDs in a CASE-shaped Curriculum Graph. Academics approve the graph once; every format reads from it and none may add truth to it.
2. **Formats are data; slot types are code.** A versioned **Format Spec** declares which graph nodes to select, which beat grammar to lay out from a closed, tested slot vocabulary, and what length, aspect, voice path, interaction and gates apply. A deterministic Derivation Compiler turns graph × spec into today's contracts (LessonPlan, SceneSpec, NarrationPlan, cue sheet). Adding a format is a data change plus one golden render.
3. **Who is learning is separated from what is made.** Level, audience safety class, persona, accessibility, subject family and locale are registry entries; a Resolver compiles one hashed **Effective Production Profile** per artefact by fixed precedence (safety beats level, level beats format, format beats style; gates add up and never drop; an empty duration intersection is a compile error). Every approval binds to that hash.

**Non-negotiables carried forward:** accuracy gates scale by subject family, never by commercial value (STEM keeps executable fixtures; every other family uses source-grounded claim ledgers, a four-state contested-claim policy and a named qualified reviewer, enforced by schema); two human approvals bound to hashes; seek-deterministic rendering; model-agnostic Production Kit and cascade; cost ledgers; the storyboard gate; AI-voice disclosure; made-for-kids rules for Class 1–5; everything versioned and hash-bound with re-QA on registry change. A CI lint rejects any commercial term in any pipeline schema or registry.

CE-L1 commercially agnostic · CE-L2 Path A for Detailed, flagship and all Class 1–5 content, Path B for derivative formats · CE-L3 ultracode banned except the one attended probe · CE-L4 pinned Linux render container; macOS for development · CE-L5 launch locales en-IN, Hinglish, hi-IN; any locale via Locale Profiles; Indic languages human-voiced until a TTS passes the native gate · CE-L6 Kit-first, strong models offline, cascade per scene, pinned model IDs · CE-L7 HyperFrames default, Remotion benchmarked, aspect is a Format Spec field · CE-L8 accuracy gates by subject family · CE-L9 made-for-kids rules for Class 1–5 · CE-L10 everything versioned and hash-bound.

## How it was produced

Four Sonnet 5.5 research angles (format taxonomies of the best providers; pedagogy by stage and child safety; validating non-STEM content; content-ops maturity at scale) → 41 sources, 20 read, 76 of 80 claims verified → four Opus 5.5 architects, one lens each → three Sonnet 5.5 judges → Opus 5.5 synthesis → Opus completeness critic → revised final. 35 agents. Then, on 15 Oct 2026, an Opus 5.5 review and fix pass (55 agents: 14 Opus reviewers with one lens each, 28 verifiers, 10 fixers with disjoint file ownership, one stitcher, one final verifier and one completeness critic) confirmed 205 findings, applied 278 fixes, added six schemas and recorded CE-73 to CE-110 (`REVIEW_opus_pass_2026-10-15.md`). A critic pass (7 agents) then applied the completeness critic's twelve operational items: CE-111 to CE-122, P1-14 to P1-17, open items O-23 and O-24, and the `curriculum_framework` and `learning_signal` schemas. 97 agents in all.
