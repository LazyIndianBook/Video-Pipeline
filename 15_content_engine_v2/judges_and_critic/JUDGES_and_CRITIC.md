# Judge panel and completeness critic (Content Engine v2)

Judges (Sonnet 5.5): a chief academic officer; a platform engineer who scaled a content factory; a child-safety and accessibility specialist. Critic (Opus 5.5) reviewed v1; its must-fix list was applied in the final version.

## Judges

### Judge 1

**Winner:** curriculum-and-subject-generalization-first

The hardest problem in "every level, every subject, every format" is keeping the content correct. Formats are comparatively cheap to add once truth is separated from packaging. The curriculum-first plan makes the objective the unit of truth. It gives each subject family a real validation profile: a claim ledger, citation rules, a contested-claim policy and a hard "no qualified reviewer, no activation" rule. Derived formats may cite only approved claims, so accuracy cost scales with objectives x families rather than with videos. It is also the smallest to build, because the registries start as versioned JSON files with no new services.

Its weaknesses are fixable by transplant. Take the slot-type vocabulary, spec linter and precedence-merge rules from format-engine-first, so formats stay data and a Class 1 request cannot silently override a safety floor. Take Audience Safety Classes, the flash and accessibility floors, the labelled-hypothesis discipline and the counsel gate from safety-first, before any Class 1-5 work. Take the supersede-versus-revoke distinction from platform-maturity, subject to the lead confirming this reading of CE-L10. Do not adopt that plan's derivative-sampling proposal or its Postgres orchestrator. Do not let derived formats skip full-watch review. Build in this order: schemas and the zero-change STEM port, the curriculum graph for one chapter, derived formats, the first non-STEM family, then young learners and higher education.

### Judge 2

**Winner:** format-engine-first

Format-engine-first wins narrowly, 40 against 39 for the curriculum-first plan. It is the only plan that makes new formats cheap and safe at the same time. A canonical Lesson Graph is approved once, and formats are versioned specs over a closed slot vocabulary. A non-weakening linter and a fixed precedence merge mean no spec can undercut a level or family floor. Approved Simplification nodes close the gap the curriculum-first plan leaves open: a derived format cannot quietly change meaning behind a closure check. Its milestone order (M0 re-derives today's output, M1 ships five formats, then locale, family, level breadth and scale) fits a small team and reuses the existing contracts, tests and gates. It still has weaknesses: heavy front-loaded graph authoring, slot-vocabulary governance, and an unresolved CE-L10 storm. Four grafts from the other plans close them. (1) From platform-maturity: the supersede versus revoke distinction by change class, plus content-addressed artefact records with a closure hash. (2) From curriculum-first: the full non-STEM Validation Profile (claim ledger, span-hash check, contested_status, qualified-reviewer rubric), the files-first CASE-shaped curriculum graph with no new service, and a hard rule that no family opens without a named qualified reviewer. (3) From pedagogy-and-safety: audience safety classes and a single compiled profile hash inside the precedence merge, with every Class 1-8 number tagged Hypothesis and report-only validators before they block. (4) From platform-maturity: maturity KPIs and cost ledger queries, adopted late and not before content exists.

### Judge 3

**Winner:** pedagogy-by-stage-and-safety-first

From a child-safety and accessibility view, the pedagogy-by-stage-and-safety-first plan is clearly strongest, and it is not weak on generality either. It is the only plan that covers all of the following:

- A separate Audience Safety Class, kept apart from commerce.
- Concrete blocking content rules for AC0 (no data solicitation, no links, no brands, no peer pressure, no photorealistic generated children).
- The under-18 DPDP vs under-13 store-regime scope mismatch, with a counsel gate before Class 1-5 launches.
- A guardian release rule for any child talent.
- A real accessibility profile: captions and a transcript, a WCAG contrast floor, WCAG 2.3.1 as the flash floor, a reduced-motion variant, and a visual-essential flag that blocks silent visuals.
- Honest [Hypothesis] tags on every young-learner number, because the engagement evidence is adult MOOC data, plus report-only validators before they become blocking.

The other three plans handle generality or scale well but treat child safety as a few flags. Their flash thresholds and accessibility are unspecified, and the format-engine plan's GA-Delta and the platform plan's derivative-sampling both shrink human review in the area where it matters most.

The recommended synthesis uses the safety-first plan as the core (EPP, AC classes, accessibility profile, persona and safety linters, phased report-only rollout) and adds ideas from the others:

- The platform plan's supersede-vs-revoke rule for CE-L10, which fixes the re-QA storm risk.
- The curriculum plan's contested-claim statuses and its rule that a family is not activated without a named qualified reviewer.
- The format-engine plan's non-weakening spec linter and approved-Simplification nodes.

Child-directed content must never use sampled review and always needs a child-development co-sign on derived formats.

## Critic

**Verdict:** Not ready to freeze. The architecture is broadly sound and mostly agrees with CE-L1 to CE-L10, but there are four kinds of problem. (1) §3.6 contradicts CE-L10: it says model, Kit and cascade changes leave approvals and published artefacts untouched, and it has no change class for format, level, locale, voice, slot-type, safety-class or overlay version bumps. (2) CE-L2's Path A rule for flagship and first-in-series lessons cannot be applied, because no field carries that information. (3) The draft-07 lesson_graph schema has structural holes: unknown fields are accepted, fields that the formats depend on are missing, the time-bound and exam-tag rules are not enforced, and hashing is underspecified for Indic text. (4) Several format and level rules contradict each other. Schema validity could only be partly checked. The schema bundle given to me cuts off inside lesson_graph.definitions.activity, and /Users/chinmoybhuyan/Desktop/Personal/Book/Videos/ExamLeaf_Video_EdTech_Pipeline/15_content_engine_v2 is empty on disk, so I could not parse the full JSON. Format_spec, level_profile and subject_profile are not visible at all, so they are unverified. By inspection, the content_request schema and the visible part of lesson_graph are well-formed draft-07 (correct $schema URI, and if/then/not used correctly). The formats file is also cut off at spec 14 and the levels file at UG, so PG and the remaining 7 or 8 format specs could not be checked.

**Must-fix (applied):**
- CE-L10 contradiction in §3.6: the 'Production-only' class (model ID, Kit, cascade) is marked 'Unaffected' with no re-QA, but CE-L10 names model changes explicitly. Change it so every registry change queues re-QA of the affected artefacts. For production-only changes that means the automated QA plus the Kit regression, recorded on the approval; a human gate re-opens only if re-derivation changes the bytes. Also add a change-class mapping for every registry kind, which is currently missing for Format Spec, Level (non-safety fields), Locale (lexicon and glossary), Voice (non-revocation, e.g. a new disclosure text), Slot type, Safety class, Exam overlay and Persona register. Without that mapping, GR's impact preview cannot classify these changes.
- CE-L2 cannot be resolved as written. Path A is required for 'first-in-series/flagship', but the Content Request has no such field (additionalProperties:false blocks it), Series 'inherits' its voice path, and Misconception Fix ('Path A for a flagship') and Lab/Demo ('Path A for real demos') depend on per-lesson facts the Resolver never sees. Add editorial, non-commercial fields: series_position (first|subsequent), flagship (bool, set by the academic lead with a reason) and demo_kind (real|virtual). Then add resolver rule R1: first ∨ flagship ∨ real demo ∨ AC0 ⇒ Path A. Exempt these field names in the commercial-term lint.
- Graph nodes accept unknown properties. node = allOf[node_base, oneOf[...]], and none of the 14 type subschemas sets additionalProperties:false. Draft-07 has no unevaluatedProperties, so misspelled fields (e.g. fixture_id vs fixture_ids, sources vs source) and commercial fields pass validation silently. This defeats P1's schema-level guarantee and the claim citation rules. Fix by flattening: repeat the base properties in each type schema and set additionalProperties:false per type. Also, registry_ref.id pattern ^[a-z]+/[a-z0-9_.-]+$ rejects the safety/AC0, safety/AC1 and safety/AC2 IDs used in §3.1 and in approval registry_pins. Lowercase the IDs to safety/ac0 and so on.
- The formats depend on graph fields the lesson_graph schema does not define: claim `key: true` (Recap, Revision Sheet), misconception `priority` (Recap 'top MIS by priority'), worked_item `twin_of` (Worked Example faded twin), claim `contrast_dimension` (Comparison), and analogy `permitted_levels` (optional today, yet §5.1 step 1 selects ANL nodes by it). Add these fields and make analogy.permitted_levels required with minItems 1, mirroring SIM.
- Time-bound claims are not enforced (CE-28). Only claim_type exam_pattern requires as_of_date. Nothing requires review_by, and law, rates, standards and current-affairs claims have no claim_type or flag at all, so the expiry-to-stale mechanism and Exam Strategy's 'fails if review_by has passed' check can be bypassed. Add `time_bound: boolean` with if/then requiring as_of_date + review_by (+ jurisdiction for law), and require review_by for exam_pattern.
- assessment_item.exam_tags is internally inconsistent. source_id, year and board_or_exam are required even when `original: true`, so original questions must cite a fake paper. Marks also have no provenance field, so 'marks render only from the source paper or official key' cannot be enforced by the schema. Use if/then: original:true ⇒ marks forbidden and source not required; original:false ⇒ source_id required. When marks are present, require marks_source ∈ {source_paper, official_key}.
- Canonical hashing is underspecified for a multi-script corpus. 'Sorted keys, UTF-8, no whitespace' says nothing about Unicode normalization (Devanagari, Bengali-Assamese and Hinglish text can differ in NFC vs NFD), float serialization, or key-sort collation. Different tools will then produce different sha256 values for the same content, which breaks approval binding and triggers false invalidation storms. Adopt RFC 8785 (JCS) plus mandatory NFC normalization on all strings before hashing, and add a cross-language golden-hash test.
- Existing v1 assets and approvals have no migration path. The §3.5 release check recomputes closure_hash and registry_pins, which v1 approvals (pilot lessons, published Reels, the 54 frozen scene packages, existing voice assets) do not have. Every existing artefact would therefore fail release or count as unbound. Specify a one-time v1→v2 import: map class_level to level_ref, create synthetic pins for 'legacy@1.0.0', and choose either grandfathering with a recorded legacy closure or a mandatory re-QA. Also give the converter its own test.
- UG audience class is non-deterministic. The UG profile reads 'AC2. Where the audience includes under-18s (first-year intake), AC1 content rules apply', but no request or registry field expresses that condition, which breaks the pure resolve(request, registry, graph) contract and the max-restrictive principle. Either make level/ug always apply AC1 content rules, or split it into level/ug_y1 (AC1) and level/ug (AC2).
- Format and level contradictions. (a) Misconception Fix says 'L1_5 uses gentle form' and also 'L3+ uses full form', which overlap for Class 3–5. CE-42 says Class 1–5 is gentle, so restrict the full form to L6+. (b) Worked Example is 'L6–L12 core, not defined for L1_2', yet L3_5 lists 'Worked Example (simple)'. (c) The Detailed format's derivation and graph inputs require ≥1 WRK per objective, while its L1–5 variant replaces worked items with story/activity slots, so AC0 Detailed fails compilation. Make the WRK requirement level-conditional. (d) The format registry says '21 registered formats', but §5.2 lists 16 plus 'five more' and then names six, which makes 22.
- The AC0 'no flashing elements at all' rule is a blocking validator from day one (CE-35 exception), but it has no operational definition: no threshold, area or luminance rule, and the WCAG 2.3.1 source is not yet in section 09. The analyser therefore cannot be implemented deterministically. Define AC0 flash operationally (for example, zero general or red flashes under the WCAG flash definitions, plus a stricter luminance-change and area rule signed off by the child-development reviewer). Gate AC0 release on O2 as well as O1. Also note that Comparison's 'consistent colour per concept' must not make colour the only differentiator (WCAG 1.4.1). Add a colour-vision-deficiency check to a11y-check.
- Content Request takes subject_family and subject independently, and nothing checks that they agree (e.g. subject=history with family=stem_fixture would select the wrong validators and reviewers, which breaks CE-L8). Add a Resolver compatibility rule that the subject must be listed in the family entry, or derive the family from a subject registry. Also, provenance records model_id and kit_version but not effort, although CE-L6 requires explicit effort. Add a required `effort` field to the if kind=model then-clause.

**Missing (addressed):**
- Schema bundle could not be fully parsed: the input cuts off inside lesson_graph.definitions.activity, and 15_content_engine_v2/ on disk is empty. Format_spec, level_profile, subject_profile, the edge definition and the cue-sheet schema are unverified. A CI step is needed that compiles every schema with a draft-07 validator and checks the golden fixtures against them.
- Formats not covered by the registry: Practice/Drill Set (many items, no teaching); Full Paper / PYQ Solution walkthrough as a composed series; 'One-shot' full-chapter marathon (dominant in the Indian market; can be a Series composition, but no rule is given); Concept/Mind Map; audio-only/podcast output; Viva/Oral-exam prep for UG/PG; teacher-facing explainer. Also missing: competitive overlays below L9 (Olympiad, NMMS, Navodaya entry), even though AC1 explicitly covers 'competitive prep aimed at them'.
- Level gaps: no mapping for NSQF and vocational levels, diploma/polytechnic or ITI, even though the Vocational family exists; no adult/professional exam level (UPSC, CA/CS, state PSC) beyond 'overlay on UG'; the PG profile was not visible (input cut off).
- Subject family gaps: medicine and health sciences (clinical and pharmacology claims need a high-risk family with a clinician reviewer and dosage fixtures); law (jurisdiction-specific, beyond civics); engineering design and management/business at UG/PG; religious and caste-sensitive content in IKS and value education needs an explicit sensitivity reviewer; executable CS fixtures need a sandboxing and security rule.
- Locale Profile has no text-direction field. Urdu and Kashmiri are Eighth Schedule languages and are right-to-left. It also lacks a pinned HarfBuzz/shaping-engine version in the container for Indic conjuncts, a spoken-math and notation policy per locale, a Hinglish transliteration standard, and a launch statement on whether the hi-IN and Hinglish TTS voices pass GL-voice. If they do not, every hi-IN and Hinglish artefact is forced onto Path A at launch.
- Safety and legal sources still missing besides DPDP and store made-for-kids policies: India's IT Rules provisions on labelling synthetically generated content and the platform AI-disclosure rules (these affect the RD-45 disclosure for both Path A STS and Path B); voice and personality-rights consent for STS performers; music and song licensing for story.song_chant; the Survey of India map-boundary rules for history and geography maps; and a medical-advice disclaimer rule for the health family.
- Accessibility gaps: an audio-description track deliverable and format (described as a rule, but no output is specified); a caption accuracy target and a QA metric; a 16:9 caption safe zone (deferred, but Lecture Segment depends on it); spoken-math captioning; dyslexia-friendly type is only a hypothesis; sign-language profile deferred with no trigger.
- Determinism gaps: FFmpeg encoder settings that guarantee bit-identical output (thread count, x264/x265 deterministic flags or a lossless intermediate); a pinned Chrome build with GPU disabled; TTS/STS outputs are inherently non-deterministic, so the narration audio must be cached by hash as an immutable input to re-renders (implied by narration_audio_sha256 but not stated as a rule); LLM slot fills are non-deterministic, so 'reproducible' must mean replaying stored outputs.
- Approvals gaps: no Reviewer registry (qualification, family, level, locale, versioned and hash-bound). 'Named qualified reviewers' are required, but nothing says where they live or how qualification changes invalidate anything. Also missing: a delegation and backup rule when the founder or the child-development reviewer is unavailable (single points of failure in H-SB and AC0), a conflict-of-interest rule, and the counsel sign-off as a recorded gate artefact.
- Cost and capacity gaps: Path A teacher-performance studio minutes are not a capacity line. CE-L2 makes all Detailed, all first-in-series and all Class 1–5 content Path A, which is likely the binding constraint alongside reviewer minutes. The STS per-minute cost per locale and the child-development reviewer minutes for AC0 at four gates are also missing from the section 08 lines.
- Source Registry vs graph duplication: SRC nodes exist both as graph node types and as content/sources/SRC-*.json, and there is no single-writer rule for them. The edge schema was not visible, so it is unclear whether edges are hashed into subtree_sha256.
