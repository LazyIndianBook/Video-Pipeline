## 6. Addendum (founder, 16 Oct 2026, later): "Pace of the video / feel of the video is not motivating enough"

This is a distinct requirement from speed. The videos must *feel* motivating: momentum from the first second, an emotional arc (curiosity → a small effort the learner can make → a payoff the learner sees coming true → earned confidence "I can do this one"), a voice with energy that varies and lands, visual rhythm with a meaningful change every 1–3 s during explanation, no dead time, a transfer question the learner actually succeeds at, and a close that leaves the learner wanting the next step, without motivational speeches, hype or forced jokes. Every Phase B deliverable (script framework, 60 s format, narration direction, design system motion, scorecard) must state how it produces momentum and motivation and how that is measured (energy curve, change-event density, time-to-first-insight, payoff placement, close type). The judges and the scorecard get a dimension for it: **D8 Momentum and motivation** (does the learner feel pulled forward and more capable at the end?).

## 7. Addendum (founder, 16 Oct 2026, later still): shorts must win followers and customers; the scripting flow itself must change

"Our videos are not exciting and motivating enough to get customers/followers from 60-second Instagram topic explanations, and our way/flow of scripting is not super exciting to humans and students. We can make it much more improved." Consequences, binding: the scripting flow becomes a creator's process (scroll test → find the surprise → five structurally different hooks scored for stop-power and honesty → open loop → shortest path to the payoff on one persistent picture with a re-hook every 8–12 s → answerable effort beat → payoff named in one quotable line → transfer win → close with the identity line, the free-lesson line and a light claim-free follow invitation), with the seven teaching obligations as the skeleton underneath and the playbook's trust rules intact (no hype, no fake claims, no fear, no sales pitch to students). The 60-second format is designed for retention, shares and follows (first frame complete, question by 3 s, turns every 8–12 s, one shareable claim line on screen ≥ 1.5 s, a "next one" tease). Judges score D8 as "momentum, motivation and the pull to watch through, share and follow". Target numbers for hook rate, watch-through, saves and follows are hypotheses to measure, never claims.
